Your support agent answers every ticket like it's the first ticket it has ever seen. The customer explained their problem twice already (once on email, once on chat), another customer had the exact same defect last month and got it fixed, and the warranty policy that settles the whole thing is sitting in a PDF nobody retrieves.

## The problem

Draw the desk your support agent actually sits at and the failure is obvious:

<Figure src="https://storage.googleapis.com/xysq_public/blogs/customer-support-memory-layer/problem.png" alt="The problem: the reply is written blind while CRM history, policy PDFs, the helpdesk archive, and chat logs sit in disconnected silos; the same defect gets rediscovered ticket after ticket, and raw identity sprawls through the logs." />

Everything the reply needs already exists. The customer's history is in the CRM, three tabs away. The rule that settles the case is in a policy PDF nobody opens mid-ticket. Forty similar tickets sit in the helpdesk archive with their resolutions buried. And the chat logs hold all of it in raw form, names and order numbers included, which is its own quiet liability. None of these silos is wired into the moment the reply gets written, so every agent starts from a blank page, and the same defect gets rediscovered from scratch, ticket after ticket. The company learns nothing from its own support volume.

This post is the fix, as a pipeline you can copy. It's maybe 60 lines of setup code, and everything below ran live against our production API: 12 out of 12 checks passed, including "zero real identities anywhere".

## The system at a glance

Same ticket, same desk. Now look what's wired into the room:

<Figure src="https://storage.googleapis.com/xysq_public/blogs/customer-support-memory-layer/overview.png" alt="The support memory system: a product brain vault, a customer brain vault with PII masked at ingress, one reply assembled from three filtered pulls, and an emergent wiki page consolidated by the engine." />

Four blocks:

1. **The product brain** (block 01): a vault holding only what the company publishes. Specs, policies, FAQs. Notably absent: a known-issues doc. Nobody writes one.
2. **The customer brain** (block 02): every customer message from every channel, in ONE vault, not a vault per customer. Tickets pass through a mask on the way in, so the stored text never carries a name, email, or order number. Identity lives in a metadata stamp instead.
3. **The three pulls** (block 03): when a ticket comes in, the agent makes three filtered calls: this customer's history (vertical), the official record (product), and this product across all customers (horizontal).
4. **The emergent page** (block 04): the payoff. The engine consolidates repeat reports into a known-issues page on its own. Nobody types it, and thanks to the mask it can't name anyone.

One sentence governs what filtered pulls can see, and it's worth memorizing: **exclusive content never crosses; shared knowledge serves every side that earned it.** A finding derived only from bob's tickets never appears under alice's filter. A finding derived from both appears under both. Policies with no customer stamp appear under everyone's.

## The scaffolding

Before the blocks: one project, two vaults, three declared keys, four tags. Prerequisites, both one-time: `pip install git+https://github.com/xysq-ai/sdk_python_xysq.git`, and in the dashboard (app.xysq.ai, under Agents) create a project called "support", copy its id, and mint an agent API key with an **admin grant** on it (creating vaults and curating tags are admin acts; a read or write grant will 403 on the very first call).

```python
from xysq import Xysq

PROJECT_ID = "1f2a..."  # from the dashboard
client = Xysq(api_key="xysq_agent_...")

product_kb = client.vaults.create("product-kb", project_id=PROJECT_ID)
customers  = client.vaults.create("customers",  project_id=PROJECT_ID,
                                  pii_scrub=True)
```

`pii_scrub=True` on the customers vault is the mask in the diagram: every push into it gets cleaned before storage. The product brain holds public material and doesn't need it. (Needs SDK ≥ 3.6.0.)

Then declare which metadata fields are filterable, and curate the tag vocabulary the whole project shares:

```python
client.vaults.declare_meta_key(customers.vault_id, "customer_id")
client.vaults.declare_meta_key(customers.vault_id, "product")
client.vaults.declare_meta_key(product_kb.vault_id, "product")

for name in ["policy", "defect", "billing", "resolved"]:
    client.tags.create(PROJECT_ID, name)
```

Two primitives, two jobs: metadata answers *who and what it belongs to* (a filter is match-or-absent: it excludes contradicting values and keeps keyless material), tags answer *what kind of thing it is* (a tag filter is hard inclusion, and unknown tags are never silently created). Four tags is a fine start; the vocabulary is a filter surface, not a folksonomy. And notice: no `known-issue` tag. We don't know the issues yet. That's the point.

## Block 01: the product brain

<Figure src="https://storage.googleapis.com/xysq_public/blogs/customer-support-memory-layer/block-01-product.png" alt="Block 01 highlighted: the product brain vault holding only official material - return policy, spec, billing FAQ." />

Only what the company publishes. Our full fixture is three docs; here they all are, so your run reproduces ours. The return policy gets pushed with *no* product metadata on purpose: match-or-absent means it stays reachable from every product-scoped pull.

```python
kb_docs = [
    ("Return policy",
     "Returns are accepted within 30 days of delivery. Defective units are "
     "replaced free of charge at any time during the 12-month warranty; the "
     "customer does not pay return shipping for a defect.",
     None, ["policy"]),
    ("Widget Pro spec",
     "Widget Pro is the 27-inch model with the M3 display controller, "
     "firmware line 4.x. Sold since January 2026.",
     "widget-pro", []),
    ("Billing FAQ",
     "Invoices are issued on the 1st. Duplicate charges are refunded within "
     "3 business days once reported.",
     None, ["policy", "billing"]),
]
for title, content, product, tags in kb_docs:
    r = client.vaults.push(product_kb.vault_id, content, title=title,
                           metadata={"product": product} if product else None)
    if tags:
        client.tags.apply(product_kb.vault_id, r.id, add=tags)
```

There is no known-issues doc in that list, and there never will be. Watch what happens instead.

## Block 02: the customer brain, masked at the door

<Figure src="https://storage.googleapis.com/xysq_public/blogs/customer-support-memory-layer/block-02-customer.png" alt="Block 02 highlighted: the customer brain vault, with tickets passing through a PII mask at ingress before anything is stored." />

Every message, every channel, one vault, stamped with who and what. The helpdesk knows each customer's real identifiers, so it passes them as `known_pii`; they're used for masking and never stored. Our fixture is four messages from three customers:

```python
tickets = [
    ("alice", "widget-pro",
     "Alice Chen (alice.c@example.com) reported: my Widget Pro screen "
     "flickers every few minutes at 120Hz. Purchased in March. Order #A-1042.",
     ["Alice Chen", "alice.c@example.com", "#A-1042"], ["defect"]),
    ("alice", "widget-pro",
     "Alice Chen followed up on chat: the flicker on her Widget Pro is worse "
     "after the room warms up. She already tried a different cable.",
     ["Alice Chen"], ["defect"]),
    ("bob", "widget-pro",
     "Bob Fischer (bob.f@example.com) reported flicker on his March-batch "
     "Widget Pro. Resolution: support had him update to firmware 4.2; "
     "flicker persisted, so a warranty replacement was shipped. He confirmed "
     "the replacement unit is stable.",
     ["Bob Fischer", "bob.f@example.com"], ["defect", "resolved"]),
    ("carol", "widget-mini",
     "Carol Dane (carol.d@example.com) was charged twice for her Widget Mini "
     "order #C-2210. Refund of the duplicate charge requested.",
     ["Carol Dane", "carol.d@example.com", "#C-2210"], ["billing"]),
]
for customer, product, content, known, tags in tickets:
    r = client.vaults.push(customers.vault_id, content,
        metadata={"customer_id": customer, "product": product},
        known_pii=known)
    client.tags.apply(customers.vault_id, r.id, add=tags)
```

Here's what the vault actually stored for alice's first ticket, read straight back from the database in our run:

```
The customer reported: my Widget Pro screen flickers every few minutes
at 120Hz. Purchased in March. Order <redacted>.
```

No name, no email, no order number, full meaning. We asserted it hard: across every stored row in the customers vault, zero of the eight real identifiers from the fixture survived. The symptom, the batch, the firmware level, the resolution: all intact. Identity lives in the `customer_id` stamp, where filters can use it and text can't leak it. If content can't be cleaned, the push is rejected with a 422 and nothing is stored.

In production this loop is a webhook from your helpdesk, chat, and email pipeline, not hand-written fixtures. The shape is what matters: content + identity metadata + `known_pii` + one or two curated tags per message. Give indexing a minute before you pull (our run waited 60 seconds).

## Block 03: one ticket, three pulls

<Figure src="https://storage.googleapis.com/xysq_public/blogs/customer-support-memory-layer/block-03-pulls.png" alt="Block 03 highlighted: one reply assembled from three filtered pulls - vertical, product, and horizontal." />

A ticket comes in: alice, flicker, Widget Pro. Your agent (human or AI) makes three pulls.

**Vertical: this customer's history.**

```python
history = client.vaults.pull(
    customers.vault_id,
    query="screen flicker history",
    filters={"meta": {"customer_id": "alice"}},
)
for item in history:
    print(item.title, "::", item.content[:80])
```

Pull returns a list of items (each has `title`, `content`, `source`, `score`). In our live run this returned alice's two tickets, the extracted symptom facts, and (this is the part we didn't script) a distilled wiki page titled *"Widget Pro Flicker Issue"*: the engine had consolidated the repeat reports on its own. The page appears in alice's pull because her tickets helped derive it; bob's pull gets the same page for the same reason. What alice's pull does NOT contain: carol's billing issue (different customer, excluded by her stamp), and any real name at all. The whole result set reads "the customer" and `<redacted>`, because that's all the vault ever stored.

**Product: the official record.**

```python
policy = client.vaults.pull(
    product_kb.vault_id,
    query="flicker warranty replacement policy",
    filters={"meta": {"product": "widget-pro"}},
)
```

Returns the Widget Pro spec and the warranty policy (which, remember, has no product stamp and survives the filter by design). Note what it does *not* return: anything about flicker being a known problem. The official record doesn't know yet.

**Horizontal: this product across all customers.**

```python
similar = client.vaults.pull(
    customers.vault_id,
    query="how was the flicker resolved",
    filters={"meta": {"product": "widget-pro"}, "tags": ["defect"]},
)
```

Metadata and tags compose in one call: only Widget Pro sources, only the ones tagged `defect`. Our run's top item, verbatim:

```
The customer reported flicker on the March-batch Widget Pro. Resolution:
support had the customer update to firmware 4.2; flicker persisted, so a
warranty replacement was shipped.
```

That's the prior fix, fully anonymous. Carol's billing issue on a different product was correctly invisible. The agent now knows this is a repeat March-batch defect, knows the fix that actually worked, and knows the policy that authorizes it. The reply writes itself: "this is a known issue with March units; firmware 4.2 sometimes helps, but you qualify for a free warranty replacement either way."

## Block 04: the page nobody wrote

<Figure src="https://storage.googleapis.com/xysq_public/blogs/customer-support-memory-layer/block-04-emergent.png" alt="Block 04 highlighted: the emergent wiki page, consolidated by the engine from repeat reports." />

Sitting next to that raw ticket in the horizontal pull was the distilled page itself: *"Widget Pro Flicker Issue"*, consolidated from three tickets, versioned in the vault's wiki layer, and physically unable to name anyone because masking happened at the door. Nobody wrote a known-issues doc. The system noticed the pattern and wrote it.

It's not magic: repeat facts across sources are exactly what a distillation layer consolidates. Two customers reporting the same flicker on the same batch *is* the known issue; the wiki page is just that pattern made retrievable, and safe to hand to any customer-facing agent as-is.

Close the loop by pushing each resolution back:

```python
r = client.vaults.push(
    customers.vault_id,
    "Confirmed March-batch defect, same as the earlier case. Firmware 4.2 "
    "did not resolve it; warranty replacement shipped per policy. Customer "
    "confirmed fixed.",
    metadata={"customer_id": "alice", "product": "widget-pro"},
)
client.tags.apply(customers.vault_id, r.id, add=["defect", "resolved"])
```

Now the page has *two* confirmed resolutions feeding it, and the next agent's horizontal pull starts from a stronger page. When the pattern is loud enough, that page is also your signal to engineering: the support system noticed the defective batch before anyone filed it.

This works identically whether the thing making the pulls is a human agent's dashboard or an AI agent drafting replies. Same three pulls, same filters. Either way the answer comes from what your customers and docs actually said, not from what a model half-remembers.

## Honest constraints (read before you build)

- **Filters are single-vault per pull.** You can't join the customer brain and the product brain in one call; you make three pulls (they're independent, run them concurrently).
- **Shared knowledge is visible from every contributing side.** That's the design, not a leak: a finding derived from alice's and bob's tickets serves both of their filtered pulls. With masking on, that shared knowledge carries no identities anyway. Exclusive content never crosses.
- **A tag filter's scope is capped at 1,000 sources**, checked on the tag closure alone. If `defect` alone outgrows that, split the vocabulary: `defect-display`, `defect-battery`.
- **Masking has exact edges.** The deterministic guarantee covers emails, phones (10+ digits), Luhn-valid cards, IPv4/IPv6, IBANs, and every `known_pii` string; unknown prose names are handled by the model pass, best-effort. Because the rewrite isn't byte-deterministic, re-pushing identical raw content may not dedupe on a masked vault. Full details in the [PII scrubbing docs](https://docs.xysq.ai/sdk/pii-scrub).
- **Tags are applied after push** (push returns the source id, then `tags.apply`). Two calls today.

## Build it

The whole pipeline is two vaults (one masked), three declared keys, four tags, and three pulls. Our reference run: 7 documents ingested through the mask, ~60 seconds of indexing, 12 out of 12 checks passed against production, including zero real identities in any stored row or any served pull. Docs for the three primitives: [tags](https://docs.xysq.ai/sdk/tags), [metadata filters](https://docs.xysq.ai/sdk/metadata-filters), and [PII scrubbing](https://docs.xysq.ai/sdk/pii-scrub), plus the [SDK getting started](https://docs.xysq.ai/sdk/getting-started).
